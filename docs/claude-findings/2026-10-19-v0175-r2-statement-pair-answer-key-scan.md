## Method

1. **SQL pre-screen.** It reads each explanation's explicit per-statement verdicts ("Statement 1 is correct…",
   "Statement 2 is false…") and compares them with the keyed choice's text, not its index, because choice
   order varies between rows.
2. **Every flagged item** went to one of two cold Opus checkers. Each judged both statements from subject
   knowledge (Philippine board-exam context, PFRS = IFRS) and derived the correct choice.
3. **A third cold checker** took a fixed random sample (`ORDER BY md5(id)`) of 50 unflagged items whose
   explanation had a verdict the screen could parse (V), and 50 whose explanation had none (N). That
   measures what the screen misses.

- **Estimated wrong keys across all 1,389 template items: about 400 (roughly 300–560), about 29%.** That is
  166 flagged and confirmed, plus 19 confirmed in the samples, plus about 217 estimated among the 1,119
  unchecked items (6% of 543 V, 32% of 576 N), extrapolated from small samples.
- **The pattern is the C7 one:** in most cases Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 false, and the key points
  to "Both" or "Only Statement 2". In 160 of the 166 confirmed cases **the explanation supports the correct
  answer**: the model reasons correctly and writes the wrong index.
- **Not code.** No app code reorders choices or moves the key on this path. The version shuffle
  (`QuizVersionShuffleUtils`) remaps it correctly, and the Challenge prompt has no answer-position rule.
- **Study Pack quizzes do not have the defect.** 0 of 432 flagged; in a fixed sample of 50 of the 1,050
  unparsed items, 48 keys are right. The 1 wrong key (`f50a214f-35d1-4796-963b-ea681981910d#2`, derivatives
  "always" at FVTPL, wrong under IFRS 9 hedge accounting) is a factual error that its explanation shares,
  not the reason-right, index-wrong pattern. The 1 ambiguous item is a loose history claim. This agrees with
  C7 (0/121 across all three models).
- **The production rate is higher than the benchmark's** (C7: mini 2/17 in quiz mode). Templates are
  20-question Official Challenge generations; the samples differ.

  B6 batch A does not add to it: it seeds packs that have no template yet, on Sol (0/42 here).

## What it means for R1 (the v0.175.0 guard)

**A check that only reads the explanation is not enough.** This screen is precise (166 of 170 flags are real,
about 98%) but catches only about 40% of wrong keys. About half the explanations state no per-statement
verdict at all, and that is where most of the misses are. The guard needs per-statement verdicts the
validator can read: for example, a schema field carrying each statement's true/false, from which the key is
derived or checked. **That is a schema and prompt change, so it needs the owner's sign-off on the exact
change** (R1 as approved).
