## Overview

Computing is the umbrella term for the study and practice of using computation and information to solve problems. It is not one subject but a family of closely related disciplines. The widely used international curriculum framework *Computing Curricula 2020* (a joint ACM and IEEE Computer Society effort) names five of them: **Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Information Systems, Information Technology and Software Engineering**.

These five share a large common foundation, yet each answers a different central question and produces a different kind of professional. Knowing the differences helps you read job titles, choose a specialization, and see where your own work fits in a larger effort.

## What the disciplines share

Every computing discipline rests on the same core ideas:

- **Algorithms and problem solving**: describing a precise procedure that solves a problem.
- **Programming**: expressing procedures so that a machine can carry them out.
- **Data and information**: representing, storing, retrieving and protecting it.
- **Computer systems and networks**: the hardware, operating software and connections that programs run on.
- **Security, ethics and professional practice**: building and operating systems responsibly.

Differences come from *emphasis*: which of these ideas a discipline treats as its centre, and what it combines them with.

## The five disciplines

### Computer Science
**Central question:** *What can be computed, and how well?*

Computer science studies computation itself: algorithms and their efficiency, the limits of what machines can compute, data structures, programming languages, and the principles behind operating systems, databases and artificial intelligence. It is the most theoretical of the five, but it is applied constantly when new methods or software foundations are created.

*Typical work:* designing algorithms, developing new software techniques, research in areas such as machine learning, language design or security.

### Computer Engineering
**Central question:** *How do hardware and software work together as one system?*

Computer engineering joins electrical engineering and computing. It covers digital logic, processor and memory design, embedded systems (computers built into devices such as vehicles, appliances and instruments), and the low-level software that controls hardware.

*Typical work:* designing circuits and processors, writing firmware for embedded devices, building hardware-software products.

### Software Engineering
**Central question:** *How do teams build large software that is reliable, maintainable and delivered on time?*

Software engineering applies engineering discipline to software. It is concerned with eliciting requirements, designing architecture, testing, managing change, working in teams and maintaining software for years. A program that one person writes in an afternoon and a system that fifty people maintain for a decade raise very different problems; software engineering addresses the second kind.

*Typical work:* leading design of a large application, defining test strategy, managing the lifecycle of a software product.

### Information Systems
**Central question:** *How can information and technology support an organization's processes and decisions?*

Information systems sits between technology and organizations. It looks at how data flows through a business or institution, how systems support operations and decision making, and how technology investments create value. Its practitioners need both technical understanding and knowledge of how organizations work.

*Typical work:* analyzing business processes, specifying and procuring systems, managing enterprise data, advising on how technology supports strategy.

### Information Technology
**Central question:** *How are computing technologies selected, deployed, integrated and kept running for the people who depend on them?*

Information technology focuses on the technology infrastructure that users and organizations rely on: networks, servers, cloud services, system administration, security operations and user support. It is the most operational of the five, concerned with making systems work well in practice and keeping them dependable.

*Typical work:* administering networks and systems, configuring and securing infrastructure, supporting users, integrating products from different vendors.

## One problem, five contributions

Consider a hospital that wants an appointment-booking system.

| Discipline | A natural contribution |
|---|---|
| Computer Science | Designs an efficient scheduling algorithm that avoids double-booking. |
| Software Engineering | Organizes the team, requirements, testing and release process for the application. |
| Information Systems | Studies how booking fits clinic workflows, billing and reporting, and what the hospital needs from the system. |
| Information Technology | Sets up and secures the servers, network and user accounts the system runs on, and supports staff. |
| Computer Engineering | Builds the check-in kiosks and the embedded devices that connect to the system. |

No single discipline could deliver the whole result alone. Real projects combine them, and one person often moves between several over a career.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Computer science is just programming."** Programming is a tool used throughout computing. Computer science itself is about computation: algorithms, data, and the limits and cost of computing.
- **"Information technology means anything involving computers."** In the framework above it names a specific emphasis, the deployment and operation of technology infrastructure, even though in everyday speech "IT" is used loosely.
- **"The disciplines are separate silos."** They overlap heavily. The same course topics, such as programming or networks, appear in several of them with different emphasis.
- **"Job titles map one-to-one to disciplines."** Titles vary between employers, and the work of a "developer" or "analyst" can draw on any of the five.
- **"One discipline is the best one."** They are different centres of gravity, suited to different interests and problems.

## Choosing and moving between disciplines

Questions that help locate your interest:

- Do you enjoy abstract problems and how efficiently they can be solved? *Computer science.*
- Do you want to work close to physical devices? *Computer engineering.*
- Do you care most about producing dependable software with a team? *Software engineering.*
- Are you drawn to how technology serves an organization's goals? *Information systems.*
- Do you like building and keeping real infrastructure running? *Information technology.*

Because of the shared foundation, moving between disciplines is common, and the boundaries between them are practical rather than strict.

## Key points to remember

- Computing is a family of disciplines with a large shared foundation.
- The five named in *Computing Curricula 2020* are Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Information Systems, Information Technology and Software Engineering.
- Each is defined by its central question and emphasis: computation (CS), hardware-software systems (CE), reliable large-scale software (SE), technology serving organizations (IS) and operating technology infrastructure (IT).
- Real projects need contributions from several disciplines.
