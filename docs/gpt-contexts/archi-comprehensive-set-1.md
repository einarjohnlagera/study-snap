Yes — **Architecture should be next**, and after looking at the actual current Architecture collection, the Civil Engineering benchmark, Claude's audit brief, and the official PRC ALE syllabus, I think this needs to be treated as a **full curriculum rebuild**, not simply “add more notes.”

Your data makes the gap pretty stark:

* Civil Engineering Comprehensive Review: **10 plans / 519 notes**
* Architect Licensure Examination Review: **5 plans / 52 notes**
* Architecture's 52 notes currently have **no sections at all**.
* There are also major ALE areas—especially **History/Theory and Architectural Practice**—that aren't represented as plans even though some of those notes already exist. 

More importantly, the official PRC syllabus gives us a much better source of truth than trying to make Architecture look structurally similar to CE. The ALE is weighted into three major areas: **History/Theory + Planning + Practice (30%)**, **Structural Design + Building Materials/Construction + Utilities (30%)**, and **Architectural Design + Site Planning (40%)**. ([Professional Regulation Commission][1])

## First: yes, I would rename it to **ALE Comprehensive Review**

I recommend:

> 🏛️ **ALE Comprehensive Review**

**Description:**
*Prepare for the Philippine Architect Licensure Examination through comprehensive review of architectural history and theory, professional practice, planning, structural concepts, building technology, utilities, architectural design, and site planning.*

I prefer this over **Architect Licensure Examination Review**.

Your rationale is correct: this collection is **not really a generic global Architecture curriculum**. We're deliberately curating it against the Philippine ALE—including RA 9266, Philippine building regulations, professional practice, and PRC syllabus coverage. RA 9266 itself defines the licensure-exam subject areas. ([Lawphil][2])

This doesn't conflict with your global strategy. The **underlying notes can remain reusable and globally discoverable** where appropriate. It's the *Review Set* that's Philippine-exam-specific.

And I'd use **ALE**, not "Architecture Licensure Examination," because PRC itself refers to it as the Architecture Licensure Examination, while the market commonly recognizes ALE. We can make the description spell out the full exam.

# What about `Architecture` vs `Architectural Engineering`?

Keep them **separate programs**.

I would **not automatically tag ALE notes `Architectural Engineering`.**

Architecture is centered on architectural design, planning, history/theory, building technology, professional architectural practice, etc.

Architectural Engineering generally places substantially greater engineering emphasis on building structural and service systems.

There will be legitimate canonical overlap:

> Building utilities → likely shared
> Building materials → often shared
> Structural fundamentals → shared selectively
> Architectural history → Architecture
> RA 9266 → Architecture
> ALE professional practice → Architecture
> Philippine architectural design → primarily Architecture

So the correct policy is **note-by-note multi-program applicability**, not treating Architectural Engineering as an alias for Architecture.

## Build order I'd use

Because you're the only curator and authoring is expensive, I would **not start creating the ~200 missing notes yet**.

First, we should do:

**Phase 1 — zero-authoring restructuring:** create the 8 children and sections; move the existing 52 notes; add the missing History and Professional Practice notes that already exist.

**Phase 2 — cheap canonical reuse:** audit Q4/Q5 and add/tag only the CE/shared notes that genuinely belong in ALE—construction materials, estimating, selected project management, structural fundamentals, etc.

**Phase 3 — remove bad Architecture tags:** clean obvious engineering-only material so the Public Library taxonomy becomes healthier at the same time.

**Phase 4 — author ALE gaps:** History → Practice → Laws → Design → Planning → Construction → Utilities → Structures, prioritizing gaps against the PRC syllabus.

That sequence could give us a **dramatically better ALE reviewer before we generate a single new note**.

And yes—I would make the rename to **🏛️ ALE Comprehensive Review** as part of this rebuild. Unlike Civil Engineering Comprehensive Review, this particular journey is explicitly calibrated to a Philippine licensure examination, so saying so in the title is clearer and more honest.
