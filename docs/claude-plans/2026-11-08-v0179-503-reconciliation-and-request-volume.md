## 2. The 2026-11-07 503 episode
**Direct:**
- Render, 60 s resolution: 06:22 to 06:24Z no requests; 06:25Z 22 requests, 2 x HTTP 503; 06:26Z 14 requests, 1 x HTTP 503; none from 06:27Z. 3 of 36 requests (8.3%) in the five-minute window; per minute 9% and 7%. The application's diagnostics file the same 3 under its minute 06:24Z (`total=41`, `statuses=[200:38,503:3]`; the two counters differ and were not reconciled).
- Of the 41 requests in that minute, 40 carried browser user-agents and 1 was `node` (the frontend server); the 9 listing requests were all `walk=none`. The sampled lines show only HTTP 200, so **the three rejected requests were not individually identified.**
- `PublicLibraryRankingSnapshotCache` (`backend/.../service/PublicLibraryRankingSnapshotCache.java`, shipped in v0.178.0): when the snapshot is older than its ceiling, requests fall back to the old SQL behind a bulkhead of 2; a third concurrent fallback throws `PublicLibraryRankingBusyException`, HTTP 503 with code `PUBLIC_LIBRARY_RANKING_BUSY`.
- Log 06:24:07Z `access result=fallback ... bulkheadRejections=0`; 06:24:14Z snapshot recomputed, `durationMs=7589`; 06:55:03Z the cumulative counter reads `bulkheadRejections=3`, and it reads 3 at every later counter line, the last at 2026-11-07T20:44:53Z. Three rejections match the three 503s.
- Request gap before the episode: almost no requests from 06:11 to 06:24Z.
**Inferred:** the snapshot was past its ceiling after that quiet gap and nine ranked listings arrived together, so the third concurrent request during the 7.6 s recompute was rejected. The snapshot's last publish time was not read.
**Recurrence:** `logCounters` returns early for cache hits and cold hits and logs every other result (fallback, rejected and so on) at most once per throttle interval (`PublicLibraryRankingSnapshotCache.java`, read at `cd6bd183`); **no counter line was found after 2026-11-07T20:44:53Z (searched through 2026-11-08T12:25Z)**, so recurrence is neither shown nor excluded by that source; Render's 5xx series shows no other 5xx since T0 through 2026-11-08T09:56Z.
**Not Stage B:** the mechanism predates Stage B (v0.178.0); a frontend rollback would not touch it. No retrospective rollback is requested.

## 3. Hard-stop interpretation (Product UX decision A)
The existing stop "Backend 5xx above 2% for 5 minutes" is interpreted **going forward** as an error rate above 2% sustained across **five consecutive one-minute observation periods**. This is a clarification of ambiguous wording, not evidence that the original intent was recorded: the wording came from the v0.178.0 plan and was copied into the Stage B plan with no definition. **Both readings are preserved for the 2026-11-07 episode:**
- **Sustained reading:** NOT triggered (two minutes of traffic above 2%, none for five).
- **Five-minute aggregate reading:** TRIGGERED (8.3% in the window; at 36 requests a single 503 is 2.8%).
Under the aggregate reading the "pause and decide" step was due at about 06:26Z and happened at the +24 h read (about 9.5 hours later; the watch cadence was hourly and 5xx was not evaluated per minute). Product UX's +24 h decision (continue, no rollback) was in effect that decision.
No new threshold and no change to the 2% value. **Monitoring going forward:** keep brief elevated-error episodes visible in each check (per-minute 5xx table), even when they do not meet the sustained duration. If that needs tooling or process changes, they are documented separately and not made during stabilization.

## 5. Approved learner-facing behaviour for the follow-up (decisions D and E)
- **Creator/profile pages fail closed:** the 120 s hard TTL; never serve expired data; on an upstream failure after expiry show the existing calm busy state, not stale content; single-flight and bounded failure backoff preserved; **no Next.js data-cache layer may bypass the hard TTL** (`lib/server-public-profiles.ts` uses `revalidate: 300` at lines 20, 45, 53; the integration needs review in the implementation plan); withdrawn or unpublished information must disappear within the bound.
- **Exam hub, review-sets request fails while notes succeed:** keep showing the notes. Section heading **"Official review sets are temporarily unavailable"**, message **"Your study notes are still available. Please try loading the review sets again in a little while."** Never show the failure as a genuine empty result, never cache it as empty data, and add no Retry control unless the UI has a working retry action. A notes/catalog failure keeps the existing fail-closed busy state.
- **Follow-up scope (one release, separate implementation authorization):** (1) creator/profile freshness; (2) signed-in back-link subject-prefetch protection **only if reproduced in a real browser** (otherwise report the evidence first); (3) exam-hub graceful degradation; (4) the stale subject-page comment. Order: this follow-up, then the pricing release, at least 24 hours between deploys, separate owner approval each. Before implementation: read the creator/profile caching code and name every stale-serving layer, verify the back link through real signed-in navigation, present the smallest backward-compatible plan and its trade-offs, define focused regression and privacy tests, plan one scoped cold falsification review, and follow the repository's routing (Codex prompt, `advisor()` before and after).

## 6. Request-volume comparison (Product UX decision F; read-only)
Method: the backend's `PUBLIC_API_DIAGNOSTICS` minute lines for the same clock hours on three days: 2026-11-06 (before the Stage B deploy), 2026-11-07 (after it, before the step) and 2026-11-08 (after the step, which began about 23:56Z on 11-07). **Every row uses one definition: Total = anonymous public GET requests (the `anonymous=` field, so authenticated and OPTIONS requests are excluded); Browser UA = Total minus `node`.** The Nov 6 sums were added by hand from the log text; the others by script. (An earlier draft of this table mixed `total=` and `anonymous=`; corrected before commit.)

| Hour (UTC) | Day | Total | Frontend server (`node`) | Browser UA | `facets` | Attributed walks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 03:00 | Nov 6 (pre-Stage B) | 90 | 81 | 9 | 0 | none |
| 03:00 | Nov 7 (post-deploy, pre-step) | 162 | 132 | 30 | 24 | subject 5 |
| 03:00 | Nov 8 (post-step) | 476 | 296 | 180 | 144 | subject 4 |
| 04:00 | Nov 6 | 117 | 108 | 9 | 0 | none |
| 04:00 | Nov 7 | 200 | 93 | 107 | 76 | exam-hub 52 |
| 04:00 | Nov 8 | 568 | 402 | 166 | 130 | exam-hub 52, subject 7 |

Frontend-server requests excluding attributed walks, the two hours added together: **Nov 6: 189 (81 + 108); Nov 7: 168 (127 + 41); Nov 8: 635 (292 + 343); about 3.4 times the Nov 6 level and 3.8 times the Nov 7 level.**
**Direct findings:**
1. The Stage B deploy did not coincide with a rise in frontend-server non-walk requests in these hours (189 before, 168 after); the rise appears on Nov 8. The cache design was identical on Nov 7 and Nov 8, so the cache design alone does not explain the step.
2. Browser-user-agent requests rose from about 9 an hour (Nov 6) to 30 and 107 (Nov 7) and 180 and 166 (Nov 8), and carry a `facets` pattern absent from both Nov 6 hours: mostly Chrome user-agents from cloud address ranges through Cloudflare, about 5 requests (4 facets, 1 listing) per client every couple of minutes. **This corrects the earlier statement made to Product UX on 2026-11-08 that browser-user-agent requests were flat: that held only from Nov 7 21Z onward, not against Nov 6.** Which public endpoints the recorder files under `facets`, and whether any NoteLib page calls them, was NOT established (a grep of the frontend source for a facets endpoint found only an unrelated comment).
3. The attributed walks are small: the 04:50Z hour on Nov 8 shows one 52-request exam-hub walk and 7 subject-walk requests; the 03Z hours show 4 to 5. Most frontend-server requests carry no walk header (`none`: 92 to 260 in these hours) and look like page-render fetches (note detail, rails).
4. Web Analytics page views (JS-executing browsers) were flat at 31 to 55 an hour through the step.
5. In the peak Render bucket (03:56 to 04:56Z, 1,006 requests) the public diagnostics account for 568 in the 04Z hour; **about 440 requests were not seen by the public diagnostics.** The database shows 352 notes created in the 02:00Z hour (none in any other hour since 18:00Z on Nov 7), which suggests bulk note generation, but the link between that run and the unaccounted requests is **unknown**.
**Hypotheses, not established:** more non-JS crawler traffic on server-rendered note pages; per-instance cache dilution multiplying fetches per page view; bulk content creation accounting for the non-public part. **Stage B is not shown to have caused the increase; the comparison does not show it did not.**
**Limitations:** one clock-hour pair per day, no variance; **the Nov 6 baseline hours sit in a quiet stretch (Render 200 responses ran 37 to 188 an hour from 2026-11-05T17Z to 2026-11-06T10Z, against 78 to 829 an hour on Nov 3 to 5, plus two spike hours of 1,044 and 1,319), so 189 may understate normal pre-Stage-B load;** the diagnostics cover anonymous public GETs only; Vercel keeps one hour of logs, so the frontend side of the pre-step days cannot be read; Render request logs for status filters returned nothing; no per-path split of the non-public requests.
**Stop rule:** Product UX's decision says to stop if the comparison is inconclusive rather than widen it. It is inconclusive on cause; the open items go into the +72 h report and nothing further is investigated without a new decision. No optimization, instrumentation, cache change or load test is authorized.
