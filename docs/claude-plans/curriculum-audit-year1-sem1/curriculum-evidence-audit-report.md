# BSCS and BSIT Year 1, first semester: curriculum evidence audit

Scope (owner direction): audit the five first-semester Subject Plans (Introduction to Computing 39 Notes, Computer Programming 1 42, Mathematics in the Modern World 21, The Contemporary World 9, Art Appreciation 7) against curriculum evidence, **not** against a Note count. Labels: **FACT**, **INFERENCE**, **PROPOSAL**.

## 1. Headline

- **FACT:** four of the five plans trace cleanly to the official or near-official requirements I could reach: Introduction to Computing, Computer Programming 1 and Mathematics in the Modern World are **ADEQUATE**.
- **The Contemporary World and Art Appreciation are not adequate.** The Notes are well-written but several topics a CHED GE course is expected to touch, including things the quoted official text names, have **no Note at all**. I confirmed absence by searching every first-semester Note body, Study Pack summary and quiz question (118 Notes). **The Contemporary World: THIN. Art Appreciation: GAP.**
- **Count is not the issue.** The first semester has more Notes (118) than the second (93 for BSIT, 95 for BSCS). What differs is distribution: the two plans with gaps are the two smallest.
- **Evidence limit that matters:** I could not read the official CMO 20 s.2013 PDF (CHED returns HTTP 403). The GE requirements below come from **Appendix A text as quoted by a newspaper columnist in 2013** (secondary quotes of the official text) plus one institutional syllabus. Every GE conclusion should be re-checked against the official PDF before any Note is authored.

### What the sources say (FACT, quoted)
- **CC100 Introduction to Computing (E1):** "an overview of the Computing Industry and Computing profession, including Research and Applications in different fields; an Appreciation of Computing in different fields such as Biology, Sociology, Environment and Gaming; an Understanding of ACM Requirements; an Appreciation of the history of computing; and Knowledge of the Key Components of Computer Systems (Organization and Architecture), Malware, Computer Security, Internet and internet protocols, HTML4/5 and CSS." Learning outcomes: explain principles and evolution of computing as they relate to different fields; the recent developments in the computing knowledge areas; analyze solutions employed by organizations.
- **CC101 (E1):** "design, implement, test, and debug programs ... using fundamental programming constructs." Outcome LO1: primitive data types, basic computation, simple I/O, conditional and iterative structures, definition of functions and parameter passing, recursion; LO2: analyze and simulate results of algorithms.
- **MMW (E2):** "Nature of mathematics, appreciation of its practical, intellectual, and aesthetic dimensions, and application of mathematical tools in daily life." Appendix A: begins with the nature of mathematics as patterns (in nature and the environment) and as inductive and deductive reasoning; then surveys "managing personal finances, making social choices, appreciating geometric designs, understanding codes used in data transmission and security, and dividing limited resources fairly."
- **The Contemporary World (E3):** "Globalization and its impact on individuals, communities and nations, challenges and responses." Appendix A: globalization "both theoretically and from the perspectives of individuals and societies affected by global firms, processes, and movements"; effects "on traditional cultures and communities, nations and political institutions, and local, national and regional economies"; students identify challenges and responses through case studies; **global civil societies engaged in advocacies relating to climate and environmental protection, human trafficking across borders, the application of advances in science and technology to serve some of the world's poorest communities**; focus "on contemporary global conditions from a Filipino perspective primarily and also as a member of the global community."
- **Art Appreciation (E4):** "Nature, function, and appreciation of the arts in contemporary society." Appendix A: "works of art ... ranging from the classical art forms to modern art installations, performance art, **indie films, enhanced e-books, and multimedia aesthetics**", examined "from an aesthetic point of view and also as reflections or critiques of the societies that produced them"; builds "understanding, critical appreciation, and expression of one's views"; ends in a written appraisal informed by "the history and tradition of art and the social milieu."

## 6. Balance of Introduction to Computing and Computer Programming 1

- **Networking, web and security in Introduction to Computing (FACT):** 12 of the 39 Notes (3 networking, 4 web, 5 security, counting the web Notes as web). That is 31% of the plan, **but the CMO 25 description names Internet and protocols, HTML and CSS, malware and computer security explicitly.** So this is **not excessive specialization**; it matches the official description. INFERENCE: "Network Security Essentials: Firewalls and VPNs" is the most specialised Note and could be argued either way; I recommend keeping it.
- **Complexity and recursion in Computer Programming 1:** recursion is covered (2 Notes, matching LO1(6)). Asymptotic notation is used in a few Computer Programming 1 Notes (for example Nested Loops, Recursion) and defined once (Big-O in "Algorithms and Their Properties"); the previously undefined Theta is now defined there. **INFERENCE:** the sequence is reasonable, but the plan says "informally" in its description while using formal notation; a curator should confirm the tone.
- **Redundancy:** I saw no duplicated Notes. Several programming Notes mention arrays, strings or pointers before Computer Programming 2 teaches them (as parameters or checks); INFERENCE: minor forward references, not a sequencing fault.
- **Cost of the two large plans:** CP1 has 22 C++-specific Notes. Under the approved C++17 decision that is intentional.

## 7. BSCS versus BSIT (independent comparison)

- **FACT:** the five first-semester plans are identical Note-for-Note in both journeys. Nothing in the first semester needs to differ: CHED names Introduction to Computing and Programming 1 as common to BSCS, BSIS and BSIT, and the GE courses are program-independent.
- **PROPOSAL:** any addition below is a **shared candidate**. If added, it should carry Computer Science and Information Technology, and it can be placed in both journeys. I recommend BSIT first, because its collection is not yet published (cheap), and BSCS in the same pass only if the owner wants the two kept identical; BSCS is currently unpublished and has no adopters (0 adopted copies, read 2026-11-05), so republishing is also cheap now.
- **No forced differences.** BSIT's own emphasis (Networking, Platform Technologies, Web Systems, Information Management) belongs to Year 2 by evidence.
