## 1. Class-B scoring protocol (changed from the earlier proposal)

| Rule | Proposal | Why, and the risk |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | Controlled synthetic fixtures built on real public notes, fixed sampling, hashed and frozen before any run; class B stays INCONCLUSIVE until the score validator exists (sections 6, 7; class-B proposal section 3). | Owner decision B1. Fixtures are authored by Codex (`gpt-6-sol`, the candidates' own family), so the report states a self-preference risk; the 10% owner spot-check (at least 3 per workflow, reserve fixtures included) is the mitigation. |
| Deterministic checks | The production parser and validators, strict schema validity, required fields nonblank, no provider control-text residue (for example a stray `</assistant:commentary>`), length recorded as a component only. | Automated and never replaced by a judgement. Covers the community-reported Luna extra-message-item shape. |
| Semantic scoring | The four approved dimensions (task fulfilled, grounded, actionable without unsupported claim, no critical misleading feedback). A failed fourth dimension is a **T2 critical** event; any other failed dimension is a **T3** deficit. | Owner decision O3. |
| **Who scores** | **The human scores ALL 66 primary class-B pairs blind** (study tip 30, collection companion 12, Ask Companion 12, Interview critique 12), pair order randomized with a recorded seed, arm key held separately. Two blind model scorers (preferably one non-GPT) score the same packet only as a second opinion; their agreement with the human is reported and no model score is ground truth. About 3.3 hours by a planning figure that is not measured. | Product UX: never treat unvalidated automated scoring as ground truth. The earlier hybrid (P2) left about 80% of agreed passes unvalidated and a 20% audit sample cannot bound the miss rate (about 8 agreeing items still leave a 95% upper bound near 31%). **Flag: full human scoring is the reliable option here and is feasible; the hybrid saves only about 1.5 hours.** |
| Reserves | Up to 30 pairs per triggered workflow. The hybrid is acceptable here only if every critical failure, every scorer disagreement and every borderline pair is human-validated, and the human re-scores the whole workflow if they change more than 1 in 5 audited items. | Load can grow to 30 pairs per triggered workflow; reserve spend needs its own approval (Stage 2). |
| Disagreement | A pair the human cannot resolve is INCONCLUSIVE, never a pass. | Missing or unresolved evidence is never a PASS (section 8). |
| Receipts | Counts, strata and SHA-256 of the manifest, score files and arm key are appended as a dated addendum before any decision; nothing private enters git. | Mirrors the Challenge Quiz preregistration. |

## 2. Failure tiers (section 8) and thresholds

| Tier | Rule | Number | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** systematic or reproducible | Provider rejects strict output or the request shape; unusable result on nearly every call (**at least 90% after at least 10 cases**); missing, unmetered or unscorable evidence. A one-off final parse or schema failure is not a blocker: the owner re-runs the same frozen input **twice**, and only a same-class final failure on both is a confirmed T1; otherwise it is investigated and counted once in T3. | 2 rechecks, 90% / 10 cases | Confirmed T1 blocks and returns to the owner; missing evidence is INCONCLUSIVE. **Correction (Addendum 3):** the ten named service suites mock the LLM, so a "named compatibility suite failure" is a regression check, not candidate evidence; the adapters' real calls are the candidate evidence. |
| **T2** critical semantic | A blind-confirmed candidate-only critical error triggers a frozen **30-pair same-workflow reserve**; **3 or more** candidate-only critical cases in the reserve is a confirmed T2 failure. A shared defect on the same case is pre-existing debt. | trigger 1, confirm 3 of 30 | Confirmed T2 blocks the route. An isolated confirmed critical incident below the threshold still needs your explicit disposition before any save. |
| **T3** noncritical | The case-level union of quality deficit, a first-attempt failure recovered by retry, an isolated unreproduced final failure, a recovered fallback, a timeout, or cost above the ceiling; candidate-only minus baseline-only pooled per route. | **D ≤ 11 / 5 / 4** for Interactive / Paid / Critique (**Interactive 11 is the Revision 2.2 change, independently verified**) | A T3-only failure is eligible for the dated Luna fallback. |

**Operating characteristics (executed, not asserted):** equivalent-candidate false-FAIL is `.1607 / .1269 / .1046` at no parse failures and stays at or under 20% through 5% (Interactive `.1862`); at 6% Interactive is `.2038`, which breaches your 20% target as in Revision 2.1. A candidate with 5 more critical-error points still passes about 10% / 50% / 59% of the time, so a clean result is a migration confidence check, not proof of a low real-world critical rate.

## 3. Sol-versus-Luna rule (section 9)

Run Sol on a route only after a Luna failure that survives investigation and confirmation. For a T3 Luna failure: Sol must be at least **3 events better** on the same pooled cases (`D(Sol,Luna) ≤ -3`) and pass its own baseline gate. For a T2 Luna failure: at least **2 fewer** confirmed candidate-only critical cases than Luna on the same reserve, plus Sol's own T2 pass. For a T1 Luna failure: that failure absent in Sol. Sol may introduce no candidate-only T1 or T2 failure anywhere, may not be run after an INCONCLUSIVE Luna result, and must clear the cost and latency checks. **Risk, not yet quantified for the new design:** the Revision 2.1 false-escalation rates (Sol statistically selected when Luna is exactly equivalent) were 10.2% / 7.3% / 5.7%, against a model about 20 times Luna's price; **recomputed for the 180-case design (done, 2026-11-13): Sol is selected on about 10.3% of exactly equivalent Interactive routes (10.315% / 10.263%, 400,000 draws per seed), essentially unchanged from the earlier 10.2%; method validated on the Revision 2.1 design within Monte Carlo noise.** Sol is a separate spending decision (Stage 3).

## 4. Cost tolerance and caps

- **Per-workflow cost ceiling: 1.25 times the owner-approved pre-generation forecast per delivered case**, applied identically to every arm; exceeding it is a T3 event, not a hard stop.
- **Stage 1 (baseline plus Luna, 246 primary cases): $12, conditionally approved.** Prices are confirmed against official OpenAI pages (Addendum 2) but the cap is **not** confirmed: medium default reasoning on Luna makes the output multiplier the binding unverified assumption; under conservative accounting the scheduled worst stays inside the $11.885 headroom only up to about 4.08 times the non-reasoning output. If the first paid call shows more, Stage 1 stops INCONCLUSIVE rather than spending past its cap. **Stage 2 ($80, triggered reserves) and Stage 3 ($55, Sol) are not approved.**
- The runner reserves each call's forecast before dispatch and keeps a durable cumulative ledger across separate arm runs; it cannot stop a response mid-flight because production sends no `max_output_tokens`.

## 5. Post-save monitoring and rollback (section 9; your attended saves only)

| Window | Watch | Rollback trigger (PROPOSED) |
|---|---|---|
| First hour | `action=llm_call` lines by operation, route, model, attempt, outcome; missing expected calls | Wrong route or model, repeatable workflow breakage, a confirmed critical wrong-key or misleading-feedback incident, or **2 final failures or timeouts among the first 10** observed cases of the route; fewer than 10 cases means hard signals only and the small denominator is reported. |
| First day | Per operation, not aggregate | Once at least 10 cases exist, a final-failure or first-attempt parse fraction more than **10 percentage points** above that operation's pre-save baseline; investigate a **25%** rise in p90 latency or cost per delivered case, and roll back if it comes with a user-visible failure. |
| Rollback | The previous value is **unset**: the three `LLM_MODEL_*` variables are not set in Render today, so a rollback is deleting the variable (back to the code default `gpt-4.1-mini` / `gpt-4.1`), an attended save and redeploy. | Possible only until **2027-01-14**; after that the old models are gone, so a supported emergency replacement must be chosen **before** then. |

Gaps to close before each save: Ask Companion, Interview Practice and the collection companion have no verified request or error visibility in the logs, and **Ask Companion has no retry wrapper**, so a parse failure surfaces as an "unavailable" error with first and final attempt identical (`OpenAiAskCompanionLlmService.java:48-71`); if the community-reported Luna extra-message item is real, the first-text extractor (`LlmResponseUtils.java:19-30`) would turn it into that error. The saves are the owner's, at least 24 hours apart, each treated as a deploy.

## 6. What approving this page does and does not do

Approving records these rules as the **Revision 2.2 protocol** and lets the release session build the class-B adapters and score validator and finish the Interactive Sol recomputation. It does **not** freeze the preregistration (that is a separate approval of the exact document at a pinned commit), authorize any paid run, the reserve spend, Stages 2 or 3, a model switch, an environment change, a merge, or a deployment.
