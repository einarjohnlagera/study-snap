### Paired operating characteristics (computed before output)

**OWNER DECISION D1, 2026-11-12:** an exactly-equivalent candidate may fail from sampling noise at most **20% per whole route**. The aggregation and all margins below remain **PROPOSED**. For every frozen case and rule family, B and C are baseline and candidate binary adverse outcomes. A candidate-only adverse outcome scores `+1`, a baseline-only adverse outcome `-1`, and a concordant pair `0`; sum these discordances as D. The **PROPOSED noncritical family** is a case-level union of final failure, first-attempt parse/schema failure (including recovered retries), structural/H4 rejection, omission/short delivery/deduplication, blind noncritical quality failure, timeout or Long Exam fallback, and a per-case cost-ceiling breach. The **PROPOSED critical family** is a separately adjudicated critical wrong key/unsupported answer or critical misleading feedback. A case contributes at most once per family; a critical case can also have a distinct noncritical defect. Latency p50/p90 and actual USD per delivered case are still reported; the owner-confirmed timeout is the latency-event boundary. **NOT DECIDED:** approve a fixed per-workflow USD ceiling before any generation; **PROPOSED formula:** 1.25 times a pre-generation per-case cost forecast from provider-confirmed prices and the approved workflow budget, applied identically to both arms. The operating characteristics condition on each arm having the stated event probabilities at that fixed ceiling and on executed compatibility suites with no candidate-only deterministic failure; missing evidence is INCONCLUSIVE.

**PROPOSED sensitivity model and exact calculation.** Cases and workflows are independent for this calculation; real shared prompts, topics and scorers may violate that assumption. Noncritical baseline event probability `p_b` uses the **PROPOSED grid** 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%. Critical `p_b` uses the **PROPOSED, unverified** 1%, 2% grid. An equivalent candidate has `p_c=p_b`; a regressed candidate has `p_c=p_b+Δ` for **PROPOSED Δ = 5, 10, 15, 20 percentage points**. Within-case association at equivalence uses the **PROPOSED** correlation grid `ρ=0, 0.5, 0.8` (independent, moderate, high). Mix an independent Bernoulli pair with probability `1-ρ` and a common-uniform pair with probability ρ. Thus `q11=(1-ρ)p_b p_c+ρ min(p_b,p_c)`, `q_C=p_c-q11`, `q_B=p_b-q11`, `q_0=1-q_C-q_B`; at equivalence the correlation is exactly ρ. Start `P(D=0)=1`, then convolve each case with masses `(q_B,q_0,q_C)` at `(-1,0,+1)`. The CDF of D gives all cells **exactly**, so no Monte Carlo seed is involved. With **PROPOSED N=30** per workflow and the section-3 route sizes `m=6,2,2`, option P pools all `30m` cases; option W requires each workflow to pass its own allowance. A route must pass both families. For equivalent-candidate route failure, the dependence-safe bound adds the two family failure probabilities; the joint figure below assumes **PROPOSED independence** between families. Values are computed from this recurrence and rounded to four decimals.

## 4. The decision unit and aggregation rule

The deployment controls are the **three** route-wide environment variables (`backend/src/main/resources/application.yaml:200-202`; `RELEASES.md:11-14`). Per-workflow results are evidence; the decision unit is each **route**. **OWNER DECISION D1, 2026-11-12:** an exactly-equivalent candidate's sampling-noise false-fail probability must be at most **20% per route**. The old every-workflow/every-gate zero-margin rule cannot meet it: even with only the **PROPOSED two independent event families**, at noncritical `p_b=.20`, critical `p_b=.02` and `ρ=0`, the exact false-fail probability would be **.9959** on the six-workflow route and **.8396** on either two-workflow route (section-1 recurrence, `D≤0` for every workflow). More gates make this worse.

**OWNER DECISION still open:** approve option P or W, or preregister another rule with operating characteristics before generation. Option P pools each family's paired discordances across a route, with the **PROPOSED** margins in section 1; it has the stronger broad-regression detection but can hide a workflow-local problem. Option W gives each workflow a **PROPOSED** excess-event allowance, then requires every workflow to satisfy it; it is more sensitive to a local regression, but weaker for broad noncritical regressions. Under the stated grids, the exact equivalent-candidate route-fail upper bounds are **computed .1273/.1173 for P** and **.1191/.0988 for W** on six-/two-workflow routes respectively (section 1). For a broad +20-point noncritical regression, the **computed family PASS maxima** are .0014/.1308 for P and .1906/.1939 for W; for a +20-point regression confined to one workflow, the whole-route PASS ranges in section 1 show substantial miss risk. This is the regression tradeoff the owner chooses, not a claim of equivalence.

A **split result** means a workflow appears worse while the route-level rule passes, or workflows disagree in direction; show each workflow's discordant counts and blind cases, then label the route result with that caveat. A route-level PASS permits an owner decision for that route only; a confirmed deterministic compatibility failure or missing required evidence is INCONCLUSIVE irrespective of pooled rate. Moving only passing workflows requires route-splitting code and is **new scope**. No workflow-level pass alone authorizes a route-wide environment save. The deadline handling for a FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE route is owner decision D2 in section 9.
