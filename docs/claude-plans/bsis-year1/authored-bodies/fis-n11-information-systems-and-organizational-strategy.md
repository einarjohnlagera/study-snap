```text
Information Systems and Organizational Strategy

📘 Overview
Organizations pursue strategies, which are long-term plans for achieving their goals, and information systems can support those strategies in several ways. Information systems create value when they help an organization work more efficiently, serve its goals more effectively or do things it could not do before, and they can contribute to competitive advantage when they support a strategy that sets the organization apart.

🧠 Key Idea
Information systems create value for an organization when they are chosen and used in ways that support its strategy, and new technological capabilities can in turn suggest strategic opportunities. Technology alone does not create advantage: competitors can often obtain similar systems, so advantage depends on how well a system fits the strategy and how well the organization uses it.

⚔️ Core Details:
- Organizational strategy is a long-term plan for how an organization will pursue its goals and compete. Information systems support a strategy when their capabilities match what the strategy requires.
- Information systems create value in three broad ways. Efficiency means doing the same work with less time or cost. Effectiveness means doing the right work better, such as improving quality or customer service. New capabilities means doing something that was not possible before, such as reaching customers in a new way.
- IT-business alignment means coordinating the organization's strategy, information systems and technology capabilities so they support one another. Business goals guide system priorities, while new technological capabilities may also create strategic opportunities.
- Planning for information systems can be seen as a cycle: understand the organization's strategy, identify the capabilities it needs, decide which systems would provide them and review whether the systems deliver the intended value. The review can also show new technological capabilities that open strategic opportunities.
- Competitive advantage is a position that lets an organization perform better than its rivals. Michael Porter described three generic ways to compete: cost leadership (being the low-cost provider), differentiation (offering something distinctive that customers value) and focus (serving a narrow segment especially well).
- Consider a fictional regional courier company. A route-planning system that lowers fuel and driver costs supports a cost leadership strategy. A tracking service that lets customers see a parcel's progress supports differentiation. A service built for small online sellers supports a focus on that segment.
- A system tends to give lasting advantage when rivals find it hard to match or when the organization uses it better than they do. A tracking feature that every competitor also offers becomes an expectation rather than a distinction.
- Information systems can also fail to help a strategy, for example when money goes to systems that do not support the organization's priorities.

🎯 Why It Matters:
- Linking systems to strategy helps an organization decide which systems deserve investment and which do not.
- Seeing the three ways systems create value helps people judge what a proposed system is meant to achieve.
- Understanding that technology alone does not create advantage explains why two organizations with similar systems can get different results.
- Strategy connects to the later topics on how technology changes organizations and how systems are developed and adopted.

🧠 Quick Recall:
- Organizational strategy — a long-term plan for how an organization will pursue its goals and compete.
- IT-business alignment — coordinating business goals, information systems and technology capabilities so they support one another.
- Value from information systems — efficiency, effectiveness and new capabilities.
- Competitive advantage — a position that lets an organization perform better than its rivals.
- Generic strategies — cost leadership, differentiation and focus, three broad ways of competing described by Michael Porter.
```
