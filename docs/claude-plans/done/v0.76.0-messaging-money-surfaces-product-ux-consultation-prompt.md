# Consultation: pricing & upgrade copy for NoteLib (v0.76.0)

**For a product-UX reviewer. Self-contained — no repo access needed.**
**Written 2026-08-14. Decisions needed before implementation starts.**

---

## 1. What NoteLib is, in one paragraph

NoteLib is a notes-first study workspace used mainly by Philippine board-exam takers. A learner captures notes, generates an AI "Study Pack" from them (summary, key concepts, flashcards, quiz), and practises with several quiz modes. Curators publish **Official Review Sets** — ready-made collections of notes for a specific board exam — which learners can adopt and study directly.

**Audience mix matters for this copy:** of accounts with a profile type set, **70.9% are exam-takers** and **27.1% are students**. Teachers and professionals are negligible. So the majority reader has a real exam with a real date.

Plans are **Free / Plus / Pro**. Payment is a manual hosted checkout with **no auto-renewal** — a learner buys a pass and it lapses unless they buy another.

---

## 2. What is already decided and is NOT open for revision

These were ratified 2026-08-01 and are binding. Please design within them rather than around them.

**The core principle:** *"We sell the learning system. Features simply support that promise."* The stated analogy is Spotify Premium selling *"Listen without interruptions"* rather than offline playlists, bitrate and skips.

**The locked hierarchy**, top to bottom:

1. **Hero** — a universal emotional outcome, profile-agnostic
2. **Supporting paragraph** — explains "the learning system", still profile-agnostic
3. **Profile-specific bullets** — personalised by profile type
4. **Features** — evidence supporting the promise

**No feature ever becomes the hero anywhere.** Board Exam Mode, Companion, Adaptive Practice, Review Sets and Progress are evidence, not the promise.

**Already shipped and not up for revision** (v0.68.0, ratified wording, live now):

- Hero: **"Always know what to learn next."**
- Supporting: **"NoteLib turns your notes into a complete learning system — organized, prioritized, and ready whenever you sit down to study."**
- Plus tagline: **"Guided learning built around your notes"**
- Pro tagline: **"Your complete learning system"**

**The ratified tier ladder** — what each tier is *for*:

| Tier | Ratified placement |
|---|---|
| **Free** | **Adopt** — take an Official Review Set someone else built and study it |
| **Plus** | **Conversational assembly** — build your own study plans from your own notes, with guidance |
| **Pro** | **Adaptive planning** — the system plans and adapts around you |

**Other hard constraints:**

- **Product names never change.** "Plus" and "Pro" stay exactly that in checkout, settings, badges, receipts and support. Only taglines and framing move. (This is to avoid a split vocabulary between what marketing says and what a receipt says.)
- **Do not write live copy for the "Professional" profile.** It exists as a setting with no features behind it; copy would describe an experience that does not exist.
- **No pricing or quota changes.** Positioning only.
- **Plan-card descriptions must stay close in length.** A previous release shipped a layout regression caused by one card's description being materially longer than its siblings across four different card renderers. Treat ~85–100 characters as the band.

## 3. The trap that already caught us once — please read before proposing a Free tagline

The Free tagline is the main open item. **An outcome-framed candidate was written for it in a previous release and then reverted.**

The reason it was reverted is the specification for this round: **it had been derived from consistency with the Plus and Pro taglines**, which contradicts the ratified `Free = adopt` placement. It sounded right next to its siblings and was wrong about what Free actually is.

**So the test is: if a proposed Free tagline only reads well when placed beside Plus and Pro, it is wrong again.** It has to be true of what Free independently offers.

**One nuance that is easy to get wrong in the other direction:** `Free = adopt` is a statement about what the tier is *for*, **not a capability boundary**. Free users *can* create their own notes and generate Study Packs, within quota. Copy that implies Free is adopt-only is inaccurate.

## 4. Current state and our drafts

We have drafts. **They are a starting point, not a preference** — please disagree freely, and treat the reasoning as more useful than the wording.

### 4a. Free plan tagline — the item that is actually owed

**Currently live:** "For getting started"
*Problem: names the onboarding moment, not an outcome. Every other tier names an outcome.*

**Our draft:** **"A complete review, ready to study"**
*Reasoning: derived from `Free = adopt` — you get something already built, that you can study today. The resulting ladder reads: ready-made → yours, guided → the whole system.*

**Alternates we considered:** "Start studying, not setting up" (sharper, but defines Free by what you avoid); "Proven review material, ready when you are" (warmer, longer).

**Known wart:** "complete" would then appear in both the Free tagline and the Pro tagline ("Your complete learning **system**" — different noun, different claim, but the echo is real). "A full review, ready to study" removes it at some cost to rhythm.

**Note on vocabulary:** in this market "review" is the ordinary word for board-exam study material ("reviewer", "review centre"), so it reads naturally to the majority audience. Confirm you are comfortable with that if you are reading from outside that market.

### 4b. The "Best for" row on the pricing comparison table

This sits directly beneath a hero promising a learning system, and currently describes feature volume. It is the most visible remaining contradiction on the page.

| Tier | Currently live | Our draft |
|---|---|---|
| Free | "Light review and trying the core study loop" | "Studying from material that's already prepared" |
| Plus | "Regular study with more room to keep going" | "Building your own study plan from your notes" |
| Pro | "Focused exam preparation and mastery practice" | "Sustained preparation with a date to hit" |

*Reasoning: reframed from feature volume to learner situation. The Pro line nods to exam-takers — the 70.9% majority — without naming a feature as the reason to upgrade.*

### 4c. Free plan description — we propose changing nothing

**Currently live:** "Create notes, generate Study Packs, review Key Concepts, and practice with Quick Review."

This is a flat feature list, and that was **deliberate**: it makes no claim about where the tier boundary sits, which keeps it honest about Free users being able to create their own notes. Every outcome-framed rewrite we tried either implied Free is adopt-only (inaccurate) or became vague enough to say nothing.

**Our position: the tagline carries positioning, the description carries facts, and that is the correct division under the hierarchy.** We would like this challenged if you disagree — it is the one place we are choosing *not* to apply the initiative.

### 4d. In-app upgrade prompts — we think the scope should narrow, and want a second opinion

When a learner hits a limit, a modal appears with a button. Buttons are currently **feature-named and context-specific**: "Unlock Board Exam Mode", "Unlock Interview Practice", "Get More Study Packs", "See review timing — get Plus".

**Our read: these should stay feature-named.** The button fires at the exact moment the learner clicked that feature. Replacing it with a system-level promise ("Get your complete learning system") would make the button *less* clear and arguably misleading — a button should name what the click does.

**What we think should carry the promise instead is the copy around the button** — the modal's headline and body. There is also a small plan-card subtext inside that modal that currently reads "Train on weak areas (limited sessions)" for Plus, and **has no Free entry at all**.

**This is the question we most want answered by someone outside the codebase**, because we may be rationalising a convenient scope reduction.

## 5. What we are asking you for

1. **The Free tagline.** Accept, revise, or replace our draft — and say explicitly whether your version survives the test in §3 (true of Free on its own, not merely consistent with its siblings).
2. **The "Best for" row.** Three lines. Same treatment.
3. **The Free description.** Is "leave it factual" right, or is that us dodging the initiative on the one card where it is hardest?
4. **The upgrade-prompt question in §4d.** Should button labels stay feature-named? If yes, what should the modal headline/body do instead — and what should the Free plan-card subtext say, given it is currently missing?
5. **Anything the hierarchy implies that we have not listed.** We scoped this to "the surfaces where we ask for money" — the landing page and an exam-hub upsell are deliberately deferred to a later pass. Tell us if that split is wrong.

**Please flag any place where our drafts drift back toward selling features rather than the system** — that is the failure mode this whole initiative exists to prevent, and we are close enough to the product to miss it.

---

## 6. Practical notes

- Every tagline appears in **two** places: the pricing page and the in-app upgrade modal. Copy must work cold (a browsing visitor) and hot (a learner blocked mid-task).
- Descriptions: **~85–100 characters**, for the layout reason in §2.
- Taglines are short — the live ones run 4–6 words.
- There is **no auto-renewal**, so no copy may imply a subscription that continues on its own.
- Plain, unhyped register. The existing voice is calm and concrete; please match it rather than raising the temperature.
