# NoteLib Facebook Group Marketing Playbook

## Product Overview

NoteLib is a notes-first study workspace.

Core workflow:

```text
Upload Notes
    ↓
Generate Study Pack
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Practice
    ↓
Improve
```

Each Study Pack may include:

* Summary
* Key Concepts
* Practice Quiz
* Quick Review
* Challenge Quiz (AI-generated questions)

### Target Audiences

* LET Takers
* PNLE Takers
* CPALE Takers
* Junior High Students
* Senior High Students
* College Students
* Teachers
* Professionals

Website:

https://www.notelib.app

# Current Marketing Philosophy

We do NOT market NoteLib primarily through product advertisements.

Instead, we market through educational engagement.

People engage with a challenge first.

They discover NoteLib second.

This approach has performed significantly better than traditional product promotion.

# Content Strategy

## Educational Challenge Posts

Every post starts with a real review question.

The question is displayed directly inside the image.

Users should be able to answer immediately without leaving Facebook.

### Important Rule

Do NOT repeat the question in the post caption.

The image already contains the question.

The caption should create curiosity and context.

---

# High-Performing Post Structure

### Formula

```text
Hook
↓
Why the concept matters
↓
Real-world connection
↓
Participation prompt
```

### Example

Most people think good residential design is all about aesthetics.

But before style comes something more important: making sure spaces are safe, functional, and actually livable.

Building standards exist for a reason—and architects are expected to know them.

Would you get this one right without looking it up?

---

# What We Learned

Older posts often continue generating engagement days or weeks later.

Because of this:

Avoid phrases like:

❌ Today's challenge

Prefer:

✅ This challenge

This keeps posts evergreen.

---

# Engagement Strategy

## Phase 1: Challenge Post

Post:

* Question image
* Hook-based caption

Do NOT reveal the answer.

---

## Phase 2: Community Engagement

Encourage:

* Answer comments
* Discussions
* Friendly debates

Respond to comments whenever possible.

---

## Phase 3: Answer Reveal

After engagement accumulates:

### Reveal Structure

1. Thank participants
2. Reveal answer
3. Give short explanation
4. Link to related NoteLib note
5. Ask a follow-up question

### Example

Thanks to everyone who joined this challenge!

Many of you got it right.

Correct Answer: B

Short explanation...

Read more:

[Note URL]

Which topic should we feature next?

# Challenge Quiz Adoption Problem

## Current Observation

Many users:

* View notes
* Use Quick Review

Very few users:

* Use Challenge Quiz

---

## Hypothesis

Users understand Quick Review immediately.

Challenge Quiz value is unclear.

Users may not realize:

* It generates new questions
* It creates fresh practice opportunities
* It provides additional exam simulation

---

## Decided Approach (June 2026)

We treat this as a *value-is-unclear* (motivation) problem, not a button-placement problem. Two reinforcing moves:

### 1. Product — auto-promote Challenge at the right moment

The post-session next-step now promotes **Take a Challenge** as the *primary* next action after a strong-majority Quick Review (at most one missed concept, i.e. >= 4/5), not only a perfect 5/5. When there is a single miss, **Retry Incorrect Questions** is kept as a secondary action so the miss is not lost; two or more misses still lead with retry. This matters because our converting cohort copies a public note and lands *directly* in a Quick Review session — they bypass the note-detail page and only ever see this post-session surface.

### 2. Marketing — educate in the answer-reveal, never as a product pitch

Fold the Challenge Quiz education into the **Phase 3 answer-reveal** of our **highest-engagement LET and PNLE posts** (these keep pulling engagement for weeks). Do **not** post it as a standalone product comment and do **not** put it in the original challenge post. Skip ALE for now (low priority this cycle).

Core framing to reuse:

> Quick Review uses saved questions (recall). Challenge Quiz generates NEW AI-powered questions every time — timed, so it feels like the real exam. Fresh practice, more variety, real exam simulation.

Attach a **tightly cropped screenshot** of the Challenge Quiz button + the helper line *"Quick Review uses saved questions • Challenge Quiz generates new timed questions"* (prefer the mobile crop; crop out the status bar; screenshot from a non-admin account so the nav matches a real user). Let the helper line carry the message; one highlight on the button, no busy chrome.

### The reinforcing loop

Answer-reveal → Public Library note link → copy → Quick Review → (at >= 4/5) the product auto-nudges into Challenge Quiz. The reveal teaches the concept; the product walks them into Challenge at the right moment.

### Validate later (don't pre-optimize)

* Challenge CTA impressions vs clicks — seen-and-ignored (motivation) vs never-reached (placement)
* Return rate of converted users — if most are one-and-done, the lever is re-engagement, not buttons
* Quick Review -> Challenge conversion rate, before vs after the 5/5 -> 4/5 change

---

# Key Marketing Principle

People do not visit NoteLib because they want NoteLib.

People visit NoteLib because they want to answer questions, learn concepts, and prepare for exams.

Always lead with the learner's goal.

Never lead with the product.
